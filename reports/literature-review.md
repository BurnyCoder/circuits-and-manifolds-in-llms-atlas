# Literature review — Circuits & Manifolds Atlas

**Date:** 2026-11-27 · **Project:** Circuits & Manifolds Atlas (author: Libor "Burny" Burian) · **Companion:** [state-of-the-art-review.md](state-of-the-art-review.md) (tools, open-model artefacts, prior-art sites, open problems)

## Scope and method

The atlas presents mechanistic-interpretability findings of seven kinds — circuits, representation geometry/manifolds, neurons, SAE features, steering directions, attribution graphs and lenses — either **recomputed** on small open models or **faithfully redrawn** from closed-model papers, and for each lists open-model replications by others. This review covers the findings the atlas is planned around. For every source it gives: citation and link, model, mechanism with key numbers and section/figure anchors, evidence type, code/data and licence, and open-model replications with a verdict.

**Verification.** Each source was loaded on 2026-11-27 from its primary page (arXiv abstract + HTML, transformer-circuits.pub, Distill, Goodfire, Anthropic, LessWrong/Alignment Forum, GitHub). transformer-circuits.pub pages larger than ~10 MB were read through their arXiv copies where they exist. Licences were read from LICENSE files / the GitHub API, Hugging Face metadata, or the arXiv abstract page. Text is paraphrased; numbers are reported with the section or figure where they appear. Some long pages were read through a summarising fetch tool, so a few secondary numbers inside dense tables are marked ◐.

**Legend.**
- ✅ verified from the primary source · ◐ partially verified (abstract/listing only, or a table read imprecisely) · ⚠ not verified (search snippet only, page did not load, or identity ambiguous).
- **Evidence:** *causal* = ablation, activation/path patching, steering, weight editing, interchange intervention; *correlational* = probes, PCA, activation statistics, feature visualisation, attribution scores without intervention.
- **Replication verdicts:** *succeeded* (core claim reproduced on an open model), *partial* (some claims reproduced, others not tested or differ), *failed* (core claim not reproduced), *critique* (argues the original analysis is flawed without a full replication).
- **"Closed → redraw"** marks findings on closed models (Claude) that the atlas can only redraw from reported numbers.

## 1. Circuits

#### 1.1 A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits (Elhage, Nanda, Olsson et al., 2021) ✅
- **Source:** Transformer Circuits Thread, 22 Dec 2021, [transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework/index.html) (26 authors; no arXiv copy; no licence statement found). Companion [exercises](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/exercises/index.html); [PySvelte](https://github.com/anthropics/PySvelte) (Apache-2.0, archived); Garcon described but not released.
- **Models:** Anthropic-internal attention-only transformers with 0, 1 and 2 layers (typically 12 heads, d_head 64, context 2048; positional information added only to queries/keys). **Not released.**
- **Mechanism:** a zero-layer model is a bigram table; a one-layer attention-only model is an ensemble of bigram and "skip-trigram" terms readable from the weights. Each head factorises into a QK circuit (where to attend) and an OV circuit (what to write). Three kinds of composition (Q-, K-, V-); in the 2-layer model the only significant composition is K-composition between a layer-1 previous-token head and layer-2 **induction heads** (§ "Analyzing a Two-Layer Model"). OV eigenvalue analysis: 10 of 12 one-layer heads are significantly copying.
- **Evidence:** mostly weights-based (QK/OV matrices, eigenvalues); causal only via frozen-attention path-term ablations.
- **Open substitutes and replications:** TransformerLens ships `attn-only-1l` … `attn-only-4l` (`NeelNanda/Attn_Only_{1..4}L512W_C4_Code`, 8 heads, d_model 512; no licence tag on the HF cards) and a `head_detector` for previous-token / duplicate-token / induction heads; [ARENA_3.0](https://github.com/callummcdougall/ARENA_3.0) has an induction-head exercise (no licence file). Redwood, [*Causal scrubbing: results on induction heads*](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/j6s9H9SHrEhEfuJnq/causal-scrubbing-results-on-induction-heads) (2-layer attn-only, OpenWebText) — **partial**: the naive induction hypothesis explains only 35% of the loss, refined hypotheses 86–89%. Singh et al. ([2404.07129](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07129)) — **succeeded/extended** (induction heads in 2-layer attn-only models are diverse and additive).

#### 1.2 In-context Learning and Induction Heads (Olsson, Elhage, Nanda et al., 2022) ✅
- **Source:** Transformer Circuits Thread, 8 Mar 2022, [transformer-circuits.pub/2022/in-context-learning-and-induction-heads](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2022/in-context-learning-and-induction-heads/index.html); arXiv [2209.11895](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.11895) (Sep 2022, CC BY 4.0). No code or models released.
- **Models:** 34 Anthropic-internal models (attention-only and with MLPs, 1–6 layers; full-scale 4L/13M to 40L/13B), > 50,000 head ablations.
- **Finding:** in-context-learning score = loss at token 500 minus loss at token 50. ICL develops abruptly at roughly 2.5–5B training tokens (< 0.15 nats before, ~0.4 nats after), coinciding with induction-head formation, except in 1-layer models where induction heads cannot form (Argument 1). Six lines of argument: co-occurrence, co-perturbation ("smeared key" architecture), direct ablation, generality examples (e.g. translation and pattern-matching heads in the 40L model), mechanistic plausibility, continuity small→large.
- **Evidence (paper's own table):** "strong, causal" for small attention-only models (ablation); "medium, correlational" for large models.
- **Open-model replications:** Tom Lieberum (in the paper's comments section) — **succeeded** in GPT-2 and GPT-Neo (e.g. GPT2-XL L21H20). Tigges et al. ([2407.10827](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10827)) — **succeeded**: in Pythia induction heads emerge soon after 2×10⁹ tokens. Crosbie & Shutova ([2407.07011](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07011), code Apache-2.0) — **succeeded**: ablating the top 1–3% prefix-matching heads in Llama-3-8B / InternLM2-20B cuts ICL by up to ~32%. Bansal et al. ([2212.09095](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09095), OPT-66B) — **partial**. Yin & Steinhardt ([2502.14010](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14010), ICML 2025, 12 models incl. Pythia) — **partial challenge**: few-shot ICL depends mainly on function-vector heads, many of which start out as induction heads.

#### 1.3 Indirect Object Identification (Wang, Variengien, Conmy, Shlegeris, Steinhardt, 2022) ✅
- **Source:** *Interpretability in the Wild: a Circuit for Indirect Object Identification in GPT-2 small*, ICLR 2023 ([OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=NpsVSN6o4ul)), arXiv [2211.00593](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.00593). Code: [redwoodresearch/Easy-Transformer](https://github.com/redwoodresearch/Easy-Transformer) (MIT).
- **Model:** GPT-2 small.
- **Circuit:** arXiv v1 reports 26 heads (1.1% of head×position pairs) in 7 classes; the ICLR abstract says 28 heads. Name movers 9.6, 9.9, 10.0; negative name movers 10.7, 11.10; S-inhibition 7.3, 7.9, 8.6, 8.10; induction 5.5, 6.9; duplicate-token 0.1, 3.0; previous-token 2.2, 4.11; eight backup name movers (§3, App. F).
- **Numbers:** full-model logit difference 3.56 (IO beats S 99.3% of the time) over 100,000 prompts from 15 templates (§2). Faithfulness |F(M) − F(C)| = 0.46, i.e. the circuit reaches 87% of the model (§4). Greedy search finds incompleteness up to 3.09 (§4.1). Every node contributes ≥ 1% of the logit difference (minimality, §4.2).
- **Evidence:** causal (path patching, introduced here; mean-ablation knockouts) + correlational (attention patterns).
- **Replications / critiques:** ACDC (Conmy et al., [2304.14997](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14997), NeurIPS 2023; code MIT) — **partial**: IOI edge AUC 0.869 with KL objective, misses negative heads when optimising logit difference. Merullo et al. ([2310.08744](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08744), ICLR 2024; code MIT) — **succeeded** on GPT-2 medium (~78% head overlap with a Colored-Objects circuit; intervening on 4 heads lifts that task 49.6% → 93.7%). Tigges et al. — **succeeded** on Pythia-70M…2.8B (algorithm stable across training and scale while specific heads change; Pythia-160M has no duplicate-token heads). Miller et al. ([2407.08734](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.08734), COLM 2024) — **critique**: IOI faithfulness swings with ablation choices (edge vs node, resample vs mean, positions, prompt order); per-prompt IQR ~50%. MIB uses IOI as a benchmark task (§1.8).

#### 1.4 Grokking and modular addition (Nanda, Chan, Lieberum, Smith, Steinhardt, 2023) ✅
- **Source:** *Progress measures for grokking via mechanistic interpretability*, ICLR 2023 oral ([iclr.cc](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/oral/12572)), arXiv [2301.05217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05217). Code: [progress-measures-paper](https://github.com/mechanistic-interpretability-grokking/progress-measures-paper) and [neelnanda-io/Grokking](https://github.com/neelnanda-io/Grokking) (no licence on either).
- **Model:** 1-layer ReLU transformer (d = 128, 4 heads, MLP 512, no LayerNorm), P = 113, trained on 30% of pairs with AdamW (wd = 1), 40k full-batch epochs (§3).
- **Mechanism:** a "Fourier multiplication" algorithm: embed inputs as cos/sin(w_k a), combine via trig identities, read out cos(w_k(a + b − c)); key frequencies k ∈ {14, 35, 41, 42, 52} (§4.1). Ablating any key frequency raises loss sharply; ablating all others *lowers* loss by 70% (§4.4).
- **Progress measures:** restricted loss (only key frequencies) and excluded loss (key frequencies removed) reveal three phases: memorisation (epochs 0–1.4k), circuit formation (1.4k–9.4k), cleanup (9.4k–14k) (§5). Robust over 4 extra seeds and P = 53, 109, 401 (App. C.2).
- **Evidence:** causal (Fourier-component ablation) + weight/activation spectra.
- **Replications / extensions:** Zhong et al., *The Clock and the Pizza* ([2306.17844](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17844), NeurIPS 2023; code [fjzzq2002/pizza](https://github.com/fjzzq2002/pizza), MIT) — **partial**: standard attention gives the "Clock" algorithm, constant attention a different "Pizza" algorithm. Chughtai et al. ([2302.03025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03025)) — **succeeded/generalised** to group composition (weak but not strong universality); Stander et al. ([2312.06581](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06581)) dispute the S₅/S₆ algorithm. Gromov ([2301.02679](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02679)) — analytic periodic solution for a 2-layer MLP. Morwani et al. ([2311.07568](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.07568)) — margin-maximisation theory predicting Fourier features. In pretrained LLMs: Kantamneni & Tegmark (§3.2), Zhou et al. ([2406.03445](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03445), fine-tuned GPT-2-XL), Goodfire *Arithmetic in the Wild* (§4.4).

#### 1.5 Copy suppression (McDougall, Conmy, Rushing, McGrath, Nanda, 2023) ✅
- **Source:** *Copy Suppression: Comprehensively Understanding an Attention Head*, arXiv [2310.04625](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04625) (CC BY 4.0); published as *… Understanding a Motif in Language Model Attention Heads*, [BlackboxNLP 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.blackboxnlp-1.22). Code: [callummcdougall/SERI-MATS-2023-Streamlit-pages](https://github.com/callummcdougall/SERI-MATS-2023-Streamlit-pages) (MIT, inherited from a TransformerLens fork). The [Streamlit app](https://copy-suppression.streamlit.app/) redirected to a sign-in page (⚠ content not checked).
- **Model:** GPT-2 small head L10H7 (App. B: GPT-2 medium, dropout-free Pythia).
- **Mechanism:** when earlier layers predict a token that already appears in context, L10H7 attends to it and suppresses it, improving calibration; this explains IOI's "negative name mover" heads (§1).
- **Numbers:** OV circuit: 84.70% of vocabulary tokens have their own diagonal entry among the 10 most negative of their column (§3.1). QK circuit: 95.72% of diagonal entries are the largest in their row (§3.2, Fig. 3). Copy-suppression-preserving ablation (CSPA) explains 76.9% of L10H7's effect (KL ratio vs mean ablation on OpenWebText, §3.3; 82% / 45% under other loss metrics, App. J.2). Copy suppression by L10H7 and L11H10 explains 39% of IOI self-repair (§4.1).
- **Evidence:** causal (mean ablation, CSPA) + weights-based OV/QK analysis.
- **Open-model replications:** Tigges et al. ([2407.10827](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10827), NeurIPS 2024) — **succeeded** within IOI: copy-suppression heads appear in the IOI circuits of Pythia-160M…2.8B. Paper's App. B — **partial** (weaker in dropout-free Pythia). Campregher et al. ([2507.11809](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11809), TMLR 2025 reproducibility) — **partial/indirect**: factual-recall heads in GPT-2 and Pythia-6.9B act via general copy suppression.

#### 1.6 Greater-than (Hanna, Liu, Variengien, 2023) ✅
- **Source:** *How does GPT-2 compute greater-than?*, NeurIPS 2023, arXiv [2305.00586](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.00586). Code: [hannamw/gpt2-greater-than](https://github.com/hannamw/gpt2-greater-than) (**no licence**; Tigges et al. note the dataset also has none).
- **Model:** GPT-2 small.
- **Task:** "The ⟨noun⟩ lasted from the year XXYY to the year XX"; XX ∈ {11…17}, YY ∈ {02…98}; 10,000 examples, 490 for path patching (§2).
- **Numbers:** probability difference Σp(y > YY) − Σp(y ≤ YY) = 81.7% (SD 19.3) for GPT-2; cutoff sharpness 6.0%. Circuit: heads a5.h1, a5.h5, a6.h9, a7.h10, a8.h8, a8.h11, a9.h1 move YY forward; MLPs 9–11 compute greater-than (MLP 8 mostly indirectly); circuit alone gives 72.7% (89.5% of the original) (§3.2–3.3). No single MLP10 neuron computes it (§4.2). Generalises to "started in … ended in" (98.8% loss recovery) and price ranges (88.9%); fails on "17YY is smaller than 17" (§5, App. F).
- **Evidence:** causal (path patching against "01" corruption; LEACE/PCA ablation) + correlational (logit lens, PCA, neuron analysis).
- **Replications / re-discovery:** ACDC ([2304.14997](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14997)) — **succeeded** (68 of 32,000 edges selected, all in the manual circuit). Dunefsky, Chlenski, Nanda, *Transcoders Find Interpretable LLM Feature Circuits* ([2406.11944](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11944)) — **succeeded** on GPT-2 small (attribution concentrates on MLP10 transcoder features fed through a9.h1). Goodfire CLT replication (§2.7) — **partial**. Tigges et al. — **partial** on Pythia-70M…2.8B (behaviour appears right after induction heads, ~2×10⁹ tokens; circuit size correlates with model size, r = 0.9). Also used as a task by EAP ([2310.10348](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10348)), EAP-IG ([2403.17806](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17806)), Edge Pruning ([2406.16778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16778)) and CD-T ([2407.00886](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00886)).

#### 1.7 Docstring circuit (Heimersheim & Janiak, 2023) ✅
- **Source:** *A circuit for Python docstrings in a 4-layer attention-only transformer*, [Alignment Forum, 20 Feb 2023](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/u6KXXmKFbXfWzoAXn/a-circuit-for-python-docstrings-in-a-4-layer-attention-only) (SERI MATS, mentored by N. Nanda). [Colab](https://colab.research.google.com/drive/17CoA1yARaWHvV14zQGcI3ISz1bIRZKS5) (no licence); task re-implemented in the ACDC repo `acdc/docstring` (MIT).
- **Model:** `attn-only-4l` ([NeelNanda/Attn_Only_4L512W_C4_Code](https://huggingface.co/NeelNanda/Attn_Only_4L512W_C4_Code): 4 layers × 8 heads, d_model 512, trained on C4 + code).
- **Mechanism:** predicts the next `:param` name by copying the right argument from the function signature. Argument-mover heads 3.0 and 3.6; fuzzy previous-token heads 0.2, 2.0; duplicate-token heads 0.5, 1.2; head 1.4 serves as previous-token head in one sub-circuit and induction head in the other; positional head 0.4; three levels of composition.
- **Numbers:** on the modified prompts the full model picks the right argument 56% of the time (chance 17%); keeping only circuit heads gives 42%; adding 1.0 gives 48%, adding 0.1 and 2.3 as well 58%. The authors frame this as recovering "up to half" of performance.
- **Evidence:** causal (resample-ablation activation patching, positional/attention-pattern swaps, ablating all non-circuit heads); the authors did not test specificity with causal scrubbing.
- **Benchmark use:** ACDC (Table 1 task 2), EAP (where ACDC-KL beats EAP), CD-T (8-head circuit metric 2.884 vs full model 3.661; augmented 11-head circuit 3.524).

#### 1.8 Successor heads (Gould, Ong, Ogden, Conmy, 2023) ✅
- **Source:** *Successor Heads: Recurring, Interpretable Attention Heads In The Wild*, ICLR 2024 ([iclr.cc poster](https://www.iclr.cc/virtual/2024/poster/17955)), arXiv [2312.09230](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.09230). **No public code:** the cited repo returns 404, as Tigges et al. App. D also note.
- **Models:** Pythia-1.4B (main; head L12H0), Pythia-2.8B, GPT-2 small/large/XL, Llama-2-7B (only 7B; App. B.2 says it generalises poorly there), Stanford GPT-2 medium; successor heads appear from 31M to 12B parameters.
- **Mechanism:** the head's effective OV circuit W_U W_OV MLP0(W_E) increments ordinal tokens (numbers, number words, days, months, letters, …) for most tokens over 8 tasks (§2, Table 1). SAEs on MLP0 output find **mod-10 features**; the modal feature is shared by 58.5% of tokens in its class (§3.2). A linear probe has cosine 0.708 with these features and gets 94/102 unseen tasks right (§3.3). Vector arithmetic works for 53% of month and 89% of digit (20–29) cases (§3.4). L12H0 is "interpretably polysemantic": resample ablation splits its loss reduction into succession 33%, acronyms 11%, greater-than 10%, copying 11% (App. K).
- **Evidence:** weights-based OV analysis; causal (direct-effect and resample ablation, steering with mod-10 vectors); correlational (SAEs, probes).
- **Replications:** Anthropic [Circuits Updates, Sep 2024](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2024/september-update/index.html) — **succeeded** (an 18-layer model's top head maps ~80% of ordinal tokens to their successor). Tigges et al. — **succeeded** on Pythia (successor heads emerge with greater-than; specific heads change role during training, e.g. Pythia-160M head 5.9 loses and 11.0 gains succession).

**Benchmark reuse.** ACDC uses IOI, docstring, greater-than, induction and two Tracr tasks; EAP uses IOI, docstring, greater-than; EAP-IG and Edge Pruning use greater-than; CD-T uses IOI, greater-than and docstring. **MIB ([2504.13151](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13151), Apache-2.0 code [aaronmueller/MIB](https://github.com/aaronmueller/MIB)) uses none of these four circuits**; its tasks are IOI, arithmetic, MCQA and ARC (+ RAVEL for causal variables). Copy suppression and successor heads are not benchmark tasks anywhere checked.

#### 1.9 Curve detectors and curve circuits (Cammarata, Goh, Carter et al., 2020–2021) ✅
- **Source:** *Curve Detectors*, Distill, 17 Jun 2020, [distill.pub/2020/circuits/curve-detectors](https://distill.pub/2020/circuits/curve-detectors/); *Curve Circuits*, Distill, **30 Jan 2021** (URL path says 2020), [distill.pub/2020/circuits/curve-circuits](https://distill.pub/2020/circuits/curve-circuits/). Text and diagrams CC-BY 4.0 (Reuse sections). Colab notebooks linked from the articles; sources [post--circuits-curve-detectors](https://github.com/distillpub/post--circuits-curve-detectors) and [post--circuits-curve-circuits](https://github.com/distillpub/post--circuits-curve-circuits) (no licence files); tools [lucid](https://github.com/tensorflow/lucid) / [lucent](https://github.com/greentfrapp/lucent) (Apache-2.0).
- **Model:** InceptionV1 (ImageNet); curve families in conv2d2 (11), mixed3a (11), mixed3b (10), mixed4a (12); focus on the 10 mixed3b neurons (3b:379, 406, 385, 343, 342, 388, 324, 340, 330, 349).
- **Findings:** 3b:379 fires on ~11% of positions (the article also says 10%). Hand-labelled images separate cleanly by activation; curves + imperfect curves make up 55% of the contribution to expected value. Synthetic curves drive activations up to 24σ above the dataset mean; the family jointly tiles all orientations. *Curve Circuits*: the > 50,000-parameter circuit reduces to a short algorithm (equivariance cuts complexity ~50×); removing negative early-curve→curve weights makes "echoes" reappear; a hand-written "artificial artificial neural network" approximates natural tuning; ablating all 3b curve neurons drops top-5 accuracy 88.6% → 86.3% on 2,000 validation images and swapping in the hand-written neurons restores 87.6% (explicitly preliminary).
- **Evidence:** *Curve Detectors* mostly correlational (dataset examples, blind labelling, tuning curves, synthetic stimuli); *Curve Circuits* causal (weight editing, hand-set replacement, ablation).
- **Replications:** Gorton, *The Missing Curve Detectors of InceptionV1* ([2406.03662](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03662)) — **succeeded/extended** (SAEs find additional curve features). [Lucent PR #51](https://github.com/greentfrapp/lucent/pull/51) — **partial** (PyTorch InceptionV1 needed corrected LRN parameters to match the synthetic-curve plots; PR closed, not shown merged). Kim et al. ([2509.17401](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17401)) — **qualitative success** in a supervised ViT. No replication of the hand-written circuit found.

#### 1.10 High-low frequency detectors (Schubert, Voss, Cammarata, Goh, Olah, 2021) ✅
- **Source:** Distill, 27 Jan 2021, [distill.pub/2020/circuits/frequency-edges](https://distill.pub/2020/circuits/frequency-edges/) (CC-BY 4.0; no code repo).
- **Model:** InceptionV1 mixed3a; universality check in AlexNet, InceptionV4, ResNetV2-50.
- **Findings:** neurons that fire where high-frequency texture on one side meets low frequency on the other, jointly covering 360°. The text says fifteen neurons, Fig. 1 lists 14 unit IDs. Built from two conv2d2 clusters (one-sided NMF yields an HF factor and an LF factor arranged spatially). 8 of the top 20 mixed3b neurons by weight norm are boundary detectors. Analogous detectors sit at 29–33% depth in other networks.
- **Evidence:** correlational/weights-based (feature visualisation, synthetic tuning curves, NMF on expanded weights); no ablation.
- **Replications:** none independent found.

#### 1.11 Multimodal neurons (Goh, Cammarata, Voss et al., 2021) ✅
- **Source:** *Multimodal Neurons in Artificial Neural Networks*, Distill, 4 Mar 2021, [distill.pub/2021/multimodal-neurons](https://distill.pub/2021/multimodal-neurons/) (CC-BY 4.0). Code: [distillpub/post--multimodal](https://github.com/distillpub/post--multimodal); [openai/CLIP-featurevis](https://github.com/openai/CLIP-featurevis) (no licence; archived 29 May 2026); [openai/CLIP](https://github.com/openai/CLIP) (MIT).
- **Model:** CLIP ResNet vision tower, final conv layer, focus on RN50-x4.
- **Findings:** in a 50-neuron sample, 76% interpretable (95% CI 64–88%). Sparse linear probe with ~3 neurons per class: 56.4% ImageNet top-5. Typographic attacks: up to 97% success changing ~7% of pixels on 1,000 validation images.
- **Evidence:** mostly correlational (faceted feature visualisation, dataset examples, blind labelling); causal via typographic attacks and emotion-neuron activation tests.

Gandelsman et al. ([2406.04341](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04341), CLIP ViT-B/32) — **related/partial**. Hufe et al., *Dyslexify* ([2508.20570](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.20570)) — typographic vulnerability **reproduced** in OpenCLIP ViT-B…bigG but localised to attention heads. No clean replication of the RN50-x4 neuron families in OpenCLIP found.

#### 1.12 Othello-GPT world model (Li et al. 2023; Nanda, Lee, Wattenberg 2023) ✅
- **Sources:** Li, Hopkins, Bau et al., *Emergent World Representations: Exploring a Sequence Model Trained on a Synthetic Task*, ICLR 2023 oral, arXiv [2210.13382](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13382); code [likenneth/othello_world](https://github.com/likenneth/othello_world) (MIT). Nanda, Lee, Wattenberg, *Emergent Linear Representations in World Models of Self-Supervised Sequence Models*, BlackboxNLP 2023, arXiv [2309.00941](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00941); code [ajyl/mech_int_othelloGPT](https://github.com/ajyl/mech_int_othelloGPT) (MIT). Essay: K. Li, [*Do Large Language Models learn world models or just surface statistics?*](https://thegradient.pub/othello/) (The Gradient, 21 Jan 2023; accessible framing, no new numbers).
- **Model:** 8-layer, 8-head, 512-d GPT trained on Othello move sequences (championship and 20M synthetic games).
- **Findings (Li):** illegal-move rate 0.01% (synthetic) vs 93.29% untrained (§2). Linear probes for black/white/empty never go below ~20% error; 2-layer MLP probes reach 1.7% (Tables 1–2). Gradient-based activation edits from layer 4 make the model predict moves legal for the edited board (average errors 0.12 / 0.06 vs null 2.68 / 2.59; §4.2).
- **Findings (Nanda et al.):** with a **mine/yours/empty** encoding the board is **linear**: probe accuracy 99.6% at layer 6 vs ~75% for colour encoding (Table 1). A single vector addition flips or erases a tile with errors 0.10 / 0.02 vs null 2.72 (Table 2).
- **Evidence:** probes (correlational) + activation editing / vector addition (causal).
- **Replications / follow-ups:** Hazineh et al. ([2310.07582](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07582)) — **succeeded** on a retrained model. Karvonen ([2403.15498](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.15498), COLM 2024; code MIT) — **succeeded** for chess (linear board probe 99.6%; skill-vector edit raises win rate 16.7% → 43.2%). He et al. ([2402.12201](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12201)) — **qualitative success** with SAEs on a smaller retrained model. Robert_AIZI ([LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/BduCMgmjJnCtc7jKc/research-report-sparse-autoencoders-find-only-9-180-board)) — **negative for SAEs** (a layer-6 SAE matches only 9 of 180 probe-found board features). Lin et al., [*OthelloGPT learned a bag of heuristics*](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/gcpNuEZnxAPayaKBY/othellogpt-learned-a-bag-of-heuristics-1) — board state computed by many local rules (complements the world-model picture). MetaOthello ([2602.23164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23164)) and Yuan & Søgaard ([2503.04421](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04421)) — ◐ abstract-level only.

## 2. Attribution graphs

#### 2.1 Circuit Tracing: methods (Ameisen, Lindsey, Pearce et al., 2025) ✅
- **Source:** *Circuit Tracing: Revealing Computational Graphs in Language Models*, Transformer Circuits Thread, 27 Mar 2025, [transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attribution-graphs/methods.html](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attribution-graphs/methods.html). No licence stated for the text; no CLT weights released; frontend [anthropics/attribution-graphs-frontend](https://github.com/anthropics/attribution-graphs-frontend) (MIT).
- **Models:** an internal 18-layer model ("18L") and Claude 3.5 Haiku. **Closed → redraw.**
- **Method:** a **cross-layer transcoder (CLT)**: each feature reads the residual stream at one layer (JumpReLU encoder) and writes to the MLP outputs of all later layers; trained with MSE + tanh sparsity penalty (§ building-architecture). CLTs of 300K–10M features (18L) and 300K–30M (Haiku). The **local replacement model** freezes attention patterns and normalisation denominators and adds **error nodes** (true MLP output − CLT output) so it reproduces the model's logits exactly on the prompt; the **attribution graph** is its linear-effect graph, pruned (default threshold 0.8 cuts nodes ~10× while losing ~20% of explained behaviour).
- **Numbers:** largest 18L CLT matches the model's next token on ~50% of (filtered) pretraining-style prompts (§ evaluating-model). Normalised reconstruction error ~11.5% at L0 88 (18L, 10M) and 21.7% at L0 235 (Haiku, 30M). On one example prompt, CLT vs per-layer transcoder (PLT) graph completeness 0.80 vs 0.78 and replacement score 0.61 vs 0.37 (single prompt only). Perturbation faithfulness: ~0.8 cosine one layer after a perturbation, degrading over layers; graph influence vs ablation effect Spearman 0.72 over 20 prompts. **Addition 36 + 59** (Haiku): inhibiting the "_6" feature makes the model output 98 instead of 95, inhibiting "_9" gives 91 (§ graphs-addition). Compute estimate: 2M-feature CLT on a 2B model with 1B tokens ≈ 3.8×10²⁰ FLOPs ≈ 210 H100-hours.
- **Evidence:** causal (feature inhibition/steering, constrained patching) + correlational (feature visualisations).

#### 2.2 On the Biology of a Large Language Model (Lindsey, Gurnee, Ameisen et al., 2025) ✅
- **Source:** Transformer Circuits Thread, 27 Mar 2025, [transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attribution-graphs/biology.html](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attribution-graphs/biology.html); [blog *Tracing the thoughts of a large language model*](https://www.anthropic.com/research/tracing-thoughts-language-model).
- **Model:** Claude 3.5 Haiku. **Closed → redraw.** Case studies: multi-step reasoning, poem planning, multilingual circuits, addition, medical diagnosis, entity recognition/hallucination, refusals, jailbreaks, CoT faithfulness, a hidden-goal model.
- **Dallas → Austin (§ dives-tracing):** Dallas → "Texas" and Texas + "say a capital" → "Austin", plus a direct Dallas → "say Austin" shortcut. Inhibiting "Dallas" lowers "Texas" but leaves "say a capital"; inhibiting "capital" does the reverse. Swapping Texas features for California, Georgia, British Columbia, China or the Byzantine Empire yields Sacramento, Atlanta, Victoria, Beijing, Constantinople (non-US swaps needed larger injections).
- **Rhyme planning (§ dives-poems):** for "He saw a carrot and had to grab it,", planned-word features ("rabbit", "habit") are active on the **newline** token before the second line; suppressing "rabbit" switches the ending to "habit"; steering only works at the newline. Planned-word features were found in about half of the poems studied; injecting a planned word in 25 poems made the line end with it in 70% of cases.
- **Multilingual (§ dives-multilingual):** "opposite of small" in English, French, Chinese; 20 of 27 multilingual-node features are active in all three prompts (10 of 27 survive pruning in all three graphs); antonym → synonym swap needed 6× the activation; operand and language swaps worked; English is mechanistically privileged as the default output language.
- **Addition (§ dives-addition):** two parallel paths — a low-precision magnitude path (~36 + ~60 ≈ 92) and a ones-digit lookup (_6 + _9 → ends in 5) — combine to 95. Asked to explain, the model describes the carry algorithm, which does not match the mechanism. The _6 + _9 feature also fires in unrelated contexts (journal citation years). Intervention numbers are in the methods paper (§2.1).
- **Evidence:** causal (inhibition, cross-prompt feature swaps, injection) + correlational.

#### 2.4 Jacopin (2026a): prolepsis, an open-model replication of poem planning ✅
- **Source:** É. Jacopin (sole author, Cosmic AI), *What Is the Minimum Architecture for Prolepsis? Early Irrevocable Commitment Across Tasks in Small Transformers*, arXiv [2604.15010](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15010) (v1 16 Apr 2026, v2 17 Jul 2026; v2 softens claims after COLM 2026 reviews). Licence CC BY 4.0.
- **Models:** Gemma-2-2B base and Llama-3.2-1B base, with the public mntss CLTs (`clt-gemma-2-2b-426k`, `clt-gemma-2-2b-2.5m`, `clt-llama-3.2-1b-524k`); Llama-3.1-8B only to build CounterFact patching data.
- **Finding:** replicates the Biology poetry-planning intervention with a suppress+inject position sweep (§5, Fig. 1, Table 1). On Gemma/426K, 95/136 pairs (70%, 95% CI 62–78%) have their peak effect at the **final prompt token** (2.5M CLT: 164/264 = 62.1%), against roughly 70% (n = 25) for Claude 3.5 Haiku in the original. Head L21:H5 dominates routing (§6). Skipping layers 22–25 drops rhymes to 0/10 vs 2/10 baseline (§7, Table 2). For factual recall, fact tokens are 4.5× more causal than template tokens (15.2% vs 3.4%) and planning heads share 0/5 with the factual top-10 heads (§8, Table 3). Six residual-stream steering methods reach 0% rhyme control vs 39.3% on a Paris→Berlin fact edit (§4).
- **Evidence:** causal (CLT-feature suppress+inject steering, layer-skip ablation, counterfactual patching, late contradictory injection); logit-lens readouts are correlational. The paper states it is not an attribution-graph analysis (§5).
- **Code/data:** promised on publication; no repo in v2.
- **Replication verdict:** **partial**. Planning-like early commitment replicates; the *site* does not: the v2 erratum moves the effective site from Anthropic's newline token to the final prompt token. v2 also reports an encoder-hook bug (post-MLP instead of pre-MLP) that invalidated an exploratory analysis but, per the author, no causal claim.

#### 2.5 Jacopin (2026b): stress-testing the poetry planning site ✅
- **Source:** É. Jacopin, *Planning or Improvisation? Stress-Testing the Poetry Planning Site on Open Models and Open Cross-Layer Transcoders*, arXiv [2609.18440](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18440) (16 Sep 2026; earlier version submitted to the BlackboxNLP 2026 reproducibility track). CC BY 4.0. Code: [PCfVW/poetry-planning-site](https://github.com/PCfVW/poetry-planning-site) (MIT; `paper/` CC BY 4.0).
- **Models:** seven model × transcoder cells (Table 3): Gemma-2-2B and Llama-3.2-1B with mntss CLTs; Qwen3-0.6B and Qwen3-1.7B with BlueLightAI JumpReLU CLTs (16K/20K features per layer); plus Gemma 2.5M CLT (§4.4).
- **Finding:** steering effect is concentrated at a single position in 7/7 cells, the final prompt token in 247/444 pairs (§4.1). Only the two mntss cells change behaviour (rhyme-change rate 0.482 Gemma, 0.853 Llama at strength 25) while random-direction controls stay ≤ 2.9×10⁻³ (§4.2). Plan-feature census on Gemma: 0.00% at newlines vs 0.28% at control positions (Table 5). Transcoder-free patching of the newline: 11 vs 4 of 1,260 lines change rhyme, Fisher p = 0.12 (§4.5). Best effect ratio varies by six orders of magnitude across CLTs (§4.6).
- **Evidence:** causal (steering with random-direction controls; transcoder-free activation patching); census correlational; one recount labelled post hoc.
- **Replication verdict:** reproduces the *shape* of Biology Fig. 13 on open models but **does not recover** the newline-planning mechanism (the author distinguishes "not recovered" from "refuted"). Results depend strongly on which transcoder is used.

#### 2.6 Ferrando et al. (2025): entity recognition and hallucination ✅
- **Source:** J. Ferrando, O. Obeso, S. Rajamanoharan, N. Nanda, *Do I Know This Entity? Knowledge Awareness and Hallucinations in Language Models*, ICLR 2025, arXiv [2411.14257](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14257) (arXiv non-exclusive licence). Code: [javiferran/sae_entities](https://github.com/javiferran/sae_entities) (repo page shows Apache-2.0).
- **Models:** Gemma-2-2B and 9B (base and IT) with Gemma Scope JumpReLU SAEs; Llama-3.1-8B replication in App. Q.
- **Finding:** using Wikidata entities (players 7,487; movies 10,895; cities 7,904; songs 8,448; §3), SAE latents at the last entity token separate known from unknown entities, best around layer 9 in Gemma-2-2B (§4, Fig. 2). Steering with the "unknown entity" latent yields near-100% refusal on 100 unknown-entity questions in the chat model; orthogonalising the direction out reduces refusals (§5, Fig. 3), i.e. a base-model latent is reused by chat fine-tuning. The unknown latent lowers attention from the final token to the entity; attribute-extraction heads include L18H5 and L20H3 (§6, Fig. 4). An uncertainty latent in Gemma-2B-IT (layer 13) reaches 73.2 AUROC for correct vs incorrect answers (§7).
- **Evidence:** correlational (separation scores, AUROC) and causal (steering, weight orthogonalisation, random-latent baselines).
- **Relation to closed-model work:** Biology (§ "Entity recognition and hallucinations") cites these latents as similar to its "known entity / can't answer" features and adds a circuit picture where known-entity features inhibit a default "can't answer" pathway. Biology also reports that activating unknown-name features did **not** induce refusal on its Michael Jordan prompt, a partial difference from Ferrando's near-100% steering (setups differ; our reading). No independent open-model replication found (not searched exhaustively).

#### 2.7 Goodfire (2025): greater-than with CLTs on GPT-2 small ✅

## 3. Representation geometry and manifolds

#### 3.1 Multidimensional circular features (Engels, Michaud, Liao, Gurnee, Tegmark, 2024) ✅
- **Source:** *Not All Language Model Features Are One-Dimensionally Linear* (v1 title: *Not All Language Model Features Are Linear*), ICLR 2025, arXiv [2405.14860](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14860) (CC BY 4.0). Code: [JoshEngels/MultiDimensionalFeatures](https://github.com/JoshEngels/MultiDimensionalFeatures) (MIT).
- **Models:** GPT-2 small and Mistral-7B (SAE discovery); Mistral-7B and Llama-3-8B (interventions).
- **Findings:** defines *irreducible* multi-dimensional features via a separability index and an ε-mixture index (§3.1). Clustering SAE features finds **circles for weekdays and months** (GPT-2 layer 7, Fig. 1; Mistral layers 8/16/24, Fig. 15). On "Two days from Monday is"-style tasks (Table 1): Llama-3-8B 29/49 weekdays, 143/144 months; Mistral-7B 31/49, 125/144; GPT-2 8/49, 10/144. Setting the circular coordinate on the top-5 PCA components to the clean answer nearly matches whole-layer patching in early layers (Fig. 6). "Very early/very late on Monday" moves along the circle (Fig. 9).
- **Evidence:** causal (circle-subspace patching, off-distribution radius/angle sweeps) + correlational (SAE clustering, PCA).
- **Replications:** none independent found. Manifold-steering work (§4.2–4.3) reuses the weekday/month circles in Llama-3.1-8B.
- **Atlas note:** GPT-2 small barely solves the task, so a GPT-2 recomputation can show the circle geometry but not its use.

#### 3.2 Addition on a generalised helix (Kantamneni & Tegmark, 2025) ✅
- **Source:** *Language Models Use Trigonometry to Do Addition*, arXiv [2502.00873](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00873) (CC BY 4.0); ICLR 2025 workshop poster (Reasoning and Planning for LLMs). **No code found.**
- **Models:** GPT-J-6B (main), Pythia-6.9B, Llama-3.1-8B (accuracy on 10,000 problems 80.5% / 77.2% / 98.0%).
- **Findings:** numbers 0–99 lie on a generalised helix: a linear term plus sin/cos pairs with periods T = 2, 5, 10, 100 (§4, Eq. 2). Patching only the helix fit recovers logit difference 7.21 vs 8.34 full and 6.13 for a 100-d PCA baseline (Table 1). "Clock" algorithm (§5): the a and b helices are moved to the last token, combined into an a + b helix, and read out; 20 of 448 heads give 83.9% of the attention effect; MLPs 14–18 build and 19–27 read out the helix; ~4,587 neurons (~1%) fitted with helix terms recover ~75% of performance (§5.4).
- **Evidence:** causal (activation and attribution patching, mean ablation) + correlational (Fourier analysis, regression fits).
- **Replications:** none independent found. Related: Nanda et al. (§1.4), Zhou et al. ([2406.03445](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03445)), Goodfire *Arithmetic in the Wild* (§4.4).

#### 3.3 *When Models Manipulate Manifolds* — linebreaking (Gurnee, Ameisen, Kauvar et al., 2025) ✅
- **Source:** *When Models Manipulate Manifolds: The Geometry of a Counting Task*, Transformer Circuits Thread, 21 Oct 2025, [transformer-circuits.pub/2025/linebreaks](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/linebreaks/index.html) (page > 10 MB; read via) arXiv [2601.04480](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04480) (8 Jan 2026, CC BY 4.0). Ringing-construction [Colab](https://colab.research.google.com/drive/13L51UzyNQ6SnjNZRyhWsx_QuyQ3LoosW); §8 links Neuronpedia graphs for the same prompt on Gemma-2-2B and Qwen3-4B.
- **Model:** Claude 3.5 Haiku, with a 10M-feature weakly causal crosscoder. **Closed → redraw.**
- **Findings:** a linear probe after layer 1 predicts the character count of the current line with R² 0.985; the count lives on a twisting curve in a 6-d subspace (top 6 PCs, 95% variance; Fig. 6), tiled by ~10 count features with tuning curves (Fig. 5). Off-diagonal "ringing" in probe responses comes from projecting a rippled manifold into low dimensions (§2.4–2.5). **Boundary heads** twist the count manifold onto the line-width manifold through their QK matrices (cosine ~0.25 in the residual stream vs ~1 in QK space; §3, Figs. 14–15). Characters-remaining and next-token-length manifolds are near-orthogonal, so a hyperplane decides the break (AUC 0.91; §4.1). Counting is built by 5 layer-0 heads (R² 0.93) / 11 heads in layers 0–1 (R² 0.97) (§5). Two-character insertions like "@@" create "visual illusions" that disrupt counting (§6).
- **Evidence:** causal (subspace ablation, rank-6 subspace patching, 2-d characters-remaining patching, illusion insertions) + correlational (probes, PCA, crosscoder features).
- **Open-model replication:** Sinii & Balagansky (T-Tech), [*Chasing the Counting Manifold in Open LLMs*](https://t-tech-manifolds.hf.space/) (19 Feb 2026; code [corl-team/counting_manifolds](https://github.com/corl-team/counting_manifolds), Apache-2.0) — **partial, correlational only**. On 1,000 FineWeb documents wrapped at 150 characters, peak probe R²: Llama-3.1-8B 0.791 (L5), Gemma-2-9B 0.868 (L11), Qwen3-8B 0.852 (L5), Pythia-160M 0.744 (L3), Pythia-410M 0.724 (L6), Gemma-3-4B-pt 0.491 (L18), **GPT-2 0.363 (L6)**. Replicated: a helix in the top 3 PCs (Llama: 97.1% of variance), period ~155, hill-shaped position features in GemmaScope/LlamaScope SAEs. Differs: best layer never in the first two layers. Not tested: ringing, boundary heads, characters-remaining, any intervention.
- **Counterpoint:** §3.11.

#### 3.4 Toy Models of Superposition (Elhage, Hume, Olsson et al., 2022) ✅
- **Source:** Transformer Circuits Thread, [transformer-circuits.pub/2022/toy_model](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2022/toy_model/index.html); arXiv [2209.10652](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10652) (arXiv non-exclusive licence; no licence on the transformer-circuits page). Code: [anthropics/toy-models-of-superposition](https://github.com/anthropics/toy-models-of-superposition) ([Colab](https://colab.research.google.com/github/anthropics/toy-models-of-superposition/blob/main/toy_models.ipynb); MIT; archived 18 Jun 2025).
- **Models:** toy linear and ReLU-output models x′ = ReLU(WᵀWx + b) (e.g. n = 20 features in m = 5 dimensions, importance 0.7ⁱ); an absolute-value computation model.
- **Findings:** sparse features are stored in superposition, dense ones are not; superposition onset is a first-order phase change with empirical and analytic phase diagrams. Dimensions-per-feature is "sticky" at 1 and ½. Feature dimensionality Dᵢ = ‖Wᵢ‖² / Σⱼ(Ŵᵢ·Wⱼ)² clusters at ¾ (tetrahedron), ⅔ (triangle), ½ (antipodal pair/digon), ⅖ (pentagon), ⅜ (square antiprism) — linked to the Thomson problem and uniform polytopes. Adversarial vulnerability rises > 3× as superposition forms. Computation in superposition is shown on the absolute-value model.
- **Evidence:** toy-model training + analytic derivations; no real-model causal tests.
- **Replications (toy, listed in the paper's comments section):** Redwood (K. Sachan) and T. McGrath — **succeeded**.
- **Atlas note:** fully recomputable in the browser or on CPU.

#### 3.5 Phylogeny manifold in Evo 2 (Pearce, Simon, Byun, Balsam, Goodfire, 2025) ✅
- **Source:** *Finding the Tree of Life in Evo 2*, Goodfire, 28 Aug 2025, [goodfire.com/papers/phylogeny-manifold](https://www.goodfire.com/papers/phylogeny-manifold) (no arXiv version, no code found).
- **Model:** Evo 2 (genomic model), layer 24 of 32; the page does not state 7B vs 40B. **Not a language model in the NL sense.**
- **Findings:** for 2,400+ GTDB bacterial species, distances along a K = 27 nearest-neighbour graph on embeddings roughly track phylogenetic branch-length distance (largest distances deviate). A supervised 10-d linear subspace correlates 0.98 (train) / ~0.9 (held-out clades) with phylogeny and captures ~70% of variance. The authors flag a confound: genome-wide nucleotide statistics predict subspace activations at ~0.9.

#### 3.6 Belief-state geometry (Shai, Marzen, Teixeira et al., 2024) ✅

- **Findings:** an affine regression from the final residual stream recovers the **fractal belief-state simplex** of the Mess3 hidden Markov process (MSE 0.0004; Fig. S1). For RRXOR (36 belief states), the geometry is recoverable only from all layers concatenated (Fig. 7E). #### 3.7 Space and time (Gurnee & Tegmark, 2023) ✅
- **Source:** *Language Models Represent Space and Time*, ICLR 2024, arXiv [2310.02207](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02207) (CC BY 4.0). Code/data: [wesg52/world-models](https://github.com/wesg52/world-models) (MIT).
- **Models:** Llama-2 7B/13B/70B (Pythia 160M–6.9B in §3.1).
- **Findings:** ridge probes on the last entity token; datasets World 39,585, USA 29,997, NYC 19,838 places; historical figures 37,539, artworks 31,321, headlines 28,389 (Table 1). Llama-2-70B at 60% depth, linear R²: World 0.911, USA 0.864, NYC 0.359, figures 0.835, entertainment 0.885, headlines 0.746; MLP probes add little (Table 2). Block-holdout errors rise (World 0.071 → 0.164; Table 3). Named "space/time neurons" in Llama-2-7B (App. B).

#### 3.8 Geometry of truth (Marks & Tegmark, 2023) ✅
- **Source:** *The Geometry of Truth: Emergent Linear Structure in Large Language Model Representations of True/False Datasets*, COLM 2024, arXiv [2310.06824](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06824) (CC BY 4.0). Code/data/explorer: [saprmarks/geometry-of-truth](https://github.com/saprmarks/geometry-of-truth) (no licence file).
- **Models:** LLaMA-2 7B/13B/70B **only**.
- **Findings:** patching localises truth value to the final statement token and end punctuation (§3); PCA separates true from false (§4); probes trained on larger_than + smaller_than exceed 95% on sp_en_trans for 13B/70B (§5.3). **Mass-mean probe** directions are most causal: on 13B (trained on cities + neg_cities) normalised indirect effect 0.85 (false → true) / 0.97 (true → false) vs logistic regression 0.33 / 0.52 and CCS 0.31 / 0.73 (Table 2).
- **Evidence:** causal (patching, direction addition) + correlational (PCA, probe generalisation).
- **Replication:** Bürger, Hamprecht, Nadler, *Truth is Universal* ([2407.12831](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12831), NeurIPS 2024) — **partial/extends**: a 2-d truth subspace across Gemma-7B, LLaMA-2-13B, Mistral-7B, LLaMA-3-8B explains failures on negated statements.

#### 3.9 Linear representation hypothesis and hierarchical concepts (Park, Choe, Jiang, Veitch, 2023–2024) ✅
- **Sources:** Park, Choe, Veitch, *The Linear Representation Hypothesis and the Geometry of Large Language Models*, ICML 2024, arXiv [2311.03658](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.03658), code [KihoPark/linear_rep_geometry](https://github.com/KihoPark/linear_rep_geometry). Park, Choe, Jiang, Veitch, *The Geometry of Categorical and Hierarchical Concepts in Large Language Models*, ICLR 2025 oral, arXiv [2406.01506](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01506), code [KihoPark/LLM_Categorical_Hierarchical_Representations](https://github.com/KihoPark/LLM_Categorical_Hierarchical_Representations). Both papers CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; neither repo has a licence file.
- **Models:** LLaMA-2-7B (+ Gemma-2B, App. D.2) for the LRH paper; **Gemma-2B** (not Gemma-2) + LLaMA-3-8B (App. F) for the hierarchy paper.
- **Findings (LRH):** formalises unembedding (probing) and embedding (intervention) representations and a **causal inner product** ⟨γ̄, γ̄′⟩ = γ̄ᵀCov(γ)⁻¹γ̄′ under which causally separable concepts are orthogonal (Thm 3.4); 27 concepts from BATS 3.0 etc.; adding the male⇒female vector makes "queen" top (Fig. 5); the Euclidean inner product fails for Gemma.
- **Findings (hierarchy):** in the whitened unembedding space, a parent vector is orthogonal to child − parent differences (Thm 8); categorical concepts form polytopes, natural ones simplices (Prop. 9); validated on 593 noun and 364 verb WordNet synsets with shuffled-unembedding controls (Fig. 3).
- **Evidence:** mostly correlational geometry; limited causal vector additions.
- **Critique:** Golechha, Bushnaq, Ong, Kayal, Schoots, [*Intricacies of Feature Geometry in LLMs*](https://github.com/iclr-blogposts/2025/blob/main/_posts/2025-04-28-feature-geometry.md) (ICLR 2025 blog track) — **failed to support specificity**: the same orthogonality and simplex structure appear for random or opposite concepts and in random unembeddings, attributed to whitening in high dimension.

#### 3.10 SAE feature structure (Li, Michaud, Baek et al., 2024) ✅ and representation manifolds (Modell, Rubin-Delanchy, Whiteley, 2025) ✅
- **Li et al.**, *The Geometry of Concepts: Sparse Autoencoder Feature Structure*, *Entropy* 27(4):344 (2025), arXiv [2410.19750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19750) (CC BY 4.0), code [ejmichaud/feature-geometry](https://github.com/ejmichaud/feature-geometry) (no licence). Gemma-2-2B + Gemma Scope residual SAEs. "Atom" scale: parallelogram/trapezoid crystals that sharpen after projecting out distractor directions such as word length (mostly qualitative, Fig. 1). "Brain" scale: co-occurrence lobes match geometric clusters (954σ / 74σ above null; Fig. 4). "Galaxy" scale: eigenvalue power law steepest in middle layers (slope −0.47 at layer 12; Fig. 7). Correlational only. The paper reports inconsistent L0 values (41 vs 176).
- **Modell et al.**, *The Origins of Representation Manifolds in Large Language Models*, arXiv [2505.18235](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18235) (arXiv licence), code [alexandermodell/Representation-Manifolds](https://github.com/alexandermodell/Representation-Manifolds) (BSD-3-Clause). Theory: a feature as a metric space mapped homeomorphically onto a manifold; if local cosine similarity decreases with feature distance, manifold path length is proportional to feature path length (Thm 1). Tests on OpenAI text embeddings, GPT-2 small layer-7 SAE (years: geodesic Pearson ρ = 0.99 under a log metric) and Mistral-7B layer-8 SAE (dates ρ = 0.97 with a circular metric; colours only weakly isometric). Correlational only.

#### 3.11 *When Models Don't Manipulate Manifolds* (Hindupur, Orgad, Fel, Ba, 2026) ✅
- **Source:** *When Models Don't Manipulate Manifolds: The Geometry of A Comparison Task*, arXiv [2609.37680](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37680) (29 Sep 2026, arXiv licence; Harvard/Kempner/Goodfire). Code: [Sai-Sumedh/comparison-geometry](https://github.com/Sai-Sumedh/comparison-geometry) (MIT).
- **Model:** Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, "max of two two-digit numbers".
- **Findings:** number activations are curved (Fig. 2a), but the computation uses **linear** directions: a layer-13 direction carries y₁ (interchange-intervention accuracy 0.94); head L14.H14 copies y₁ to y₂'s position; a 2-d plane before the layer-14 MLP reaches IIA 0.81 / 0.94 (Table 4); 6 layer-14 neurons compare locally and 6 layer-15 neurons combine; a DAS direction at layer 15 reaches IIA 1.00 (§2.4).
- **Evidence:** causal (interchange interventions, DAS, attribution patching, neuron freezing); claims sufficiency, not necessity (§4).
- **Relation:** a **counterpoint, not a refutation** of §3.3 — different model and task; argues curved geometry and linear computation coexist.

#### 3.12 Out of scope for an LLM atlas: the Latent Color Subspace ✅
- Pach, Bader, Bouniot … Belongie, Akata, *The Latent Color Subspace: Emergent Order in High-Dimensional Chaos*, ICML 2026, arXiv [2603.12261](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12261) (CC BY 4.0), code [ExplainableML/LCS](https://github.com/ExplainableML/LCS) (licence not found). **Model: FLUX.1 [Dev] text-to-image diffusion transformer (VAE latent space), not a language model.** Three PCs of solid-colour latents carry all colour variance, arranged as an HSL-like bicone (lightness + hue circle with saturation radius; §3.2, Fig. 2); closed-form latent interventions raise GenEval colour accuracy from 9% to 72% (Table 2). #### 4.1 *The World Inside Neural Networks* (Geiger, Lubana, Fel et al., 2026) ✅
- **Source:** [blog essay](https://www.goodfire.com/research/the-world-inside-neural-networks) (no arXiv version linked).
- **Content:** position piece, no new experiments. Argues that activation geometry mirrors the structure of the world (number/weekday/month circles in LLMs, colour surfaces in image models, a tree-of-life manifold in a genomic model, an Alzheimer's biomarker curve); that linear steering cuts through off-manifold "voids" when the geometry is curved; and that SAEs "shatter" manifolds (e.g. 23 SAE features tile a "-ore" slant-rhyme manifold while their auto-labels describe only local properties).

#### 4.2 Manifold steering (Wurgaft, Rager, Kowal et al., 2026) ✅
- **Source:** *Manifold Steering Reveals the Shared Geometry of Neural Network Representation and Behavior*, arXiv [2605.05115](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05115) (6 May 2026; arXiv non-exclusive licence; 16 authors incl. Goodman, Fel, Geiger, Lubana); [blog](https://www.goodfire.com/research/manifold-steering). An ICML 2026 listing appeared in search results (not opened). Code: [goodfire-ai/causalab@manifold_steering](https://github.com/goodfire-ai/causalab/tree/manifold_steering) (**no licence file**).
- **Models:** Llama-3.1-8B, residual stream layer 28, last token (§2.2); a small custom Mountain Car world model (conv encoder, GRU hidden 64, conv decoder; §5).
- **Method:** activation manifold = PCA to 64 dims + cubic splines through concept centroids; behaviour manifold fit in Hellinger space over output distributions (§2.2).
- **Findings:** geodesic distances on the activation manifold track behavioural distances far better than linear distances (§2.3; r geodesic vs linear: weekdays 0.99 vs 0.89, months 0.89 vs 0.53, letters 0.999 vs 0.71, ages 0.999 vs 0.36). Steering along the manifold produces more natural output paths (cumulative output "energy", lower = better, manifold vs linear: weekdays 0.34 vs 0.93, months 0.36 vs 1.09, letters 2.42 vs 6.95, ages 5.21 vs 13.49; ~2.8× on average, p < 0.001; §3.2). Optimised "pullback" interventions fit behaviour paths with R² 0.77/0.75/0.78/0.47 vs 0.42/0.32/0.23/0.24 for linear (§3.3). In-context graph tasks: geodesic r 0.99 vs linear 0.90 (grid) / 0.81 (cylinder) (§4). Mountain Car: arc-length r 0.99 vs 0.06 (§5).
- **Evidence:** causal (manifold vs linear steering; pullback interventions) + correlational isometry tests. Limitations (§8): templated tasks, next-token outputs only, manifold fitting needs concept labels.
- **Open-model replications:** Marbut, [*CLT Features Support Manifold Steering, but Hold Only Partial Steering Signal*](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/Qiy7pziBEKYZHEn26/clt-features-support-manifold-steering-but-hold-only-partial) (Gemma-2-2B, layer 20) — **succeeded** in the residual stream (energy 0.60 manifold vs 0.79 linear), **partial** in CLT feature space (mean p(target) 0.27 vs 0.54 in raw activations). Marbut, [*Manifold Steering from a Natural State…*](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/cNxsEkkXzGA8aqCJL/manifold-steering-from-a-natural-state-is-effective-and) (Gemma-2-2B) — **partial** (92% correct end-day when projected onto the manifold, but the model answers only 43.5% of the weekday-arithmetic prompts correctly). Gupta et al. (§4.3) — **challenge**.

#### 4.3 *Spokes, not a Ring* (Gupta, Fruengel, Heimersheim, 2026) ✅ — outside critique of 4.2
- **Source:** *Spokes, not a Ring: Revisiting the Weekday Manifold in Language Models*, arXiv [2610.37796](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37796) (27 Oct 2026, CC BY 4.0; Pivotal Research). Code: [dikshagup/spokes-not-a-ring](https://github.com/dikshagup/spokes-not-a-ring) (MIT).
- **Models:** Llama-3.1-8B (main), Mistral-7B-v0.1, GPT-2 XL via TransformerLens; steering at layer 2, readout at 87.5% depth.
- **Findings:** reusing Wurgaft et al.'s periodic-spline ring, Jacobian sensitivity has seven minima, one per weekday; sensitivity between days exceeds sensitivity at days by 3.71× (Llama), 6.68× (Mistral), 1.34× (GPT-2 XL), with 95% bootstrap CIs (App. B, Fig. 6). Early-layer (L2) steering gives step-like behaviour, late-layer steering looks smooth (Fig. 1, §4.1). Time of day shifts weekday points ≤ 6.30° vs 44–60° between adjacent days (§4.2.1). Under two-day uncertainty the nearest spoke is the favoured day's for 76–95% of activations (§4.2.2).
- **Evidence:** causal (steering, Jacobian sensitivity, off-ring and random-direction controls) + correlational (PCA, probes).
- **Scope:** narrower than a refutation: concedes the smooth behaviour at layer 28 but argues smoothness does not imply a continuous variable; studies only weekdays, an inherently discrete concept (§5.2).

#### 4.4 *Arithmetic in the Wild* ("Geometric Calculator"; Feucht, Haklay, Bhalla et al., 2026) ✅
- **Source:** *Arithmetic in the Wild: Llama uses Base-10 Addition to Reason About Cyclic Concepts*, arXiv [2605.01148](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01148) (1 May 2026, CC BY 4.0); [blog](https://www.goodfire.com/research/a-geometric-calculator). Code: [goodfire-ai/arithmetic-wild](https://github.com/goodfire-ai/arithmetic-wild) (no licence file).
- **Model:** Llama-3.1-8B; key computation in the layer-18 MLP at the last token.
- **Findings:** on month / weekday / 24-hour arithmetic (accuracy 82% / 92% / 97%, App. A.2), the model computes a **base-10 sum** (e.g. "six + August" → 14) and maps it back onto the cycle in later layers (§3) — it does not rotate on a months circle. Probes recover Fourier "number circles" with periods T ∈ {2, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100} (§4); the output concept's circle is not probeable at the final token at layer 18 (§2). DAS isolates inputs with > 95% accuracy before the layer-18 MLP (§2). 28 MLP neurons (~0.2% of the layer) cluster by period; zero-ablating them drops addition accuracy 95% → 24%, keeping only them gives 86% (§5.1).
- **Evidence:** causal (DAS, activation patching, Fourier-probe steering, neuron ablation). One model; the sum→concept mapping step is not analysed.
- **Relation:** consistent with the helix/Fourier picture of Kantamneni & Tegmark (§3.2) and Nanda et al. (§1.4).

#### 4.5 *Do Sparse Autoencoders Capture Concept Manifolds?* (Bhalla, Fel, Rager et al., 2026) ✅
- **Source:** arXiv [2604.28119](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.28119) (30 Apr 2026, CC BY 4.0); [blog *Can SAEs Capture Neural Geometry?*](https://www.goodfire.com/research/can-saes-capture-neural-geometry). Code: [goodfire-ai/sae-manifold](https://github.com/goodfire-ai/sae-manifold) (MIT).
- **Models:** synthetic manifolds in ℝ¹²⁸ (TopK SAEs, width 512, k = 4) and Llama-3.1-8B layer-19 residual stream with five SAE types (L1, JumpReLU, TopK, BatchTopK, Matryoshka) trained on 500M Pile tokens.
- **Findings:** an SAE can capture a manifold globally (a compact group of atoms spanning it) or locally (features tiling regions); the paper defines "shattering" and "dilution" regimes via Ising-model coupling signatures (Def. 6). In Llama, variance explained keeps rising with the number of features well beyond the manifold's intrinsic dimension, i.e. dilution (Fig. 6). An Ising-coupling pipeline on a BatchTopK SAE (×8, k = 64) recovers temperature, colour and political-bias manifolds and a new "epistemic uncertainty" manifold (Fig. 10).
- **Evidence:** correlational/reconstruction + synthetic ground truth; no causal interventions.

#### 4.6 *Stories in Space* (Bigelow, Sarfati, Wurgaft et al., 2026) ✅
- **Source:** *Stories in Space: In-Context Learning Trajectories in Conceptual Belief Space*, arXiv [2605.12412](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12412) (12 May 2026, CC BY 4.0); [blog *Meandering on Manifolds*](https://www.goodfire.com/research/stories-in-space) (23 Jun 2026); [demo](https://conceptual-beliefs.streamlit.app). No code repo found.
- **Model:** Llama-3.1-8B, probes at layer 9; SimpleStories (2,500 train / 500 test).
- **Findings:** linear probes predict the model's elicited belief ratings over six concepts per domain (RMSE 0.09 Emotions, 0.09 Genres, 0.11 arbitrary control). Behaviour and activation distance matrices correlate (r = 0.92 / 0.89 / 0.71). The emotion layout matches human valence–arousal ratings (r = 0.93). Steering needed a 7-layer span because later layers corrected single-layer edits; off-target effects follow the geometry (§5, Fig. 6).
- **Evidence:** correlational (probes, distance correlation) + causal (probe and diff-of-means steering).

#### 4.7 Block-Sparse Featurizers (Fel, Kowal, Jacobs et al., 2026) ✅
- **Source:** *Structuring Sparsity: Block-Sparse Featurizers Capture Visual Concept Manifolds*, arXiv [2606.25234](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25234) (23 Jun 2026, CC BY 4.0; 25 authors); [blog](https://www.goodfire.com/research/bsf-vision). Code: [goodfire-ai/block-sparse-featurizer](https://github.com/goodfire-ai/block-sparse-featurizer) (MIT).
- **Models:** InceptionV1, DINOv3 ViT-B (final-layer patches), SDXL, a toy model. **Vision, not language.**
- **Findings:** featurizers whose latents are small blocks (Vanilla, Grassmannian, Group-Lasso variants). Toy model per-block R² 0.93–0.97 vs oracle 0.99. On DINOv3, minimum description length is lowest at block size 2–4, below an SAE (block size 1) (Fig. 7); learned block stable rank settles at 2–4 even when 16 is allowed (Fig. 8). InceptionV1 curve block: first three Fourier harmonics explain 89% of variance (59/18/12%). DINOv3 shows shadow/lighting manifolds; SDXL steerable along manifolds.
- **Evidence:** reconstruction/MDL + causal steering (SDXL).
- **Replications:** Jerpelea & Ananthram, [*A Deeper Analysis of Block-Sparse Featurizers*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27515) (DINOv3, CLIP, toy; CC BY 4.0) — **partial**: BSFs keep manifolds as single units where SAEs fragment them, but still show feature splitting; proposes "Tournament Top-K".

## 5. Neurons as the unit of analysis

#### 5.1 Universal neurons (Gurnee, Horsley, Guo et al., 2024) ✅
- **Source:** *Universal Neurons in GPT2 Language Models*, arXiv [2401.12181](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12181) (TMLR 2024 per a mirror; ◐ OpenReview not opened). Code: [wesg52/universal-neurons](https://github.com/wesg52/universal-neurons) (MIT).
- **Models:** GPT-2 small and medium, 5 seeds each (Stanford CRFM checkpoints), plus Pythia-160M.
- **Findings:** a neuron is called universal if its maximum activation correlation with neurons in other seeds exceeds a randomly-rotated baseline by > 0.5 over 100M Pile tokens (§4.1, Eq. 3; the authors call the threshold unprincipled). Only 1.23% (GPT-2 medium), 4.16% (GPT-2 small) and 1.26% (Pythia-160M) are universal (§4.1). Families include unigram, alphabet (18/26 letters), previous-token, position, syntax and semantic neurons (§4.3); functional roles include prediction, suppression and partition neurons, plus an entropy neuron L23.945 with an anti-entropy partner L22.2882 (cosine −0.886) (§5). Path-ablating L4.3594 onto head L5.H0 increases attention to BOS, i.e. an attention-deactivation neuron (§5.3).
- **Evidence:** correlational (cross-seed correlation, weight statistics) + causal (fixed-activation interventions, path ablation).
- **Replications:** Nandan et al., [arXiv 2508.00903](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.00903) — **succeeded/extended** on five GPT-2 small seeds (universal neurons stable across checkpoints; ablating them raises loss significantly).

#### 5.2 Confidence-regulation neurons (Stolfo, Wu, Gurnee et al., 2024) ✅
- **Source:** *Confidence Regulation Neurons in Language Models*, NeurIPS 2024, arXiv [2406.16254](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16254) (CC BY 4.0). Code: [bpwu1/confidence-regulation-neurons](https://github.com/bpwu1/confidence-regulation-neurons) (no licence).
- **Models:** GPT-2 small, LLaMA-2-7B (main); Pythia-410M for frequency neurons; appendices cover GPT-2 medium, Pythia-1B, Phi-2, Gemma-2B.
- **Findings:** **entropy neurons** have large weight norm but little direct logit effect; they write into an effective null space of the unembedding (GPT-2 small's singular values drop near 0 around index 755; neurons write into the bottom k = 12 directions; §3.3–3.4) and act by rescaling the final LayerNorm. About 80% of their effect is mediated by LayerNorm in GPT-2, LLaMA-2-7B and Phi-2, ~40% in Pythia and Gemma (App. A). **Token-frequency neurons** shift logits along the log-unigram direction (e.g. Pythia-410M neuron 23.417; §4). Clipping GPT-2 small neuron 11.2378 cuts entropy by up to 70% on induction sequences, consistent with hedging (§6.1).
- **Evidence:** causal (total vs direct-effect mean ablation, clipping, BOS ablation of induction head L5H1) + weight geometry.
- **Replications by others:** none found.

#### 5.3 Neuron circuits are sparse (Arora, Wu, Steinhardt, Schwettmann, 2026) ✅
- **Source:** *Language Model Circuits Are Sparse in the Neuron Basis*, ICML 2026 Spotlight, arXiv [2601.22594](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22594) (CC BY 4.0). Code: [TransluceAI/circuits](https://github.com/TransluceAI/circuits) (no licence per GitHub API).
- **Models:** Llama-3.1-8B base (subject–verb agreement) and Instruct (multi-hop); Gemma-2-2B/9B with Gemma Scope SAEs (App. E); Llama Scope 8× SAEs as baseline.
- **Findings:** nodes are MLP hidden activations (pre-down-projection), attributed with RelP (one backward pass; compared to integrated gradients). Neuron circuits are ~100× smaller than MLP-output circuits (§5.1, Fig. 1). ~200 neurons reach near-perfect faithfulness and completeness on subject–verb agreement, closing the gap to SAEs (§5.2, Fig. 2; qualitative comparison). > 80% faithfulness with ~10⁵ edges (§5.4). On Dallas → Texas → Austin, tracing finds 257 neurons (23 judged key, 6 clusters); steering L23/N8079 negatively flips capital → state answers in a majority of examples (§6).
- **Evidence:** causal (mean ablation for faithfulness/completeness; steering).
- **Follow-up:** same group's ADAG, arXiv [2604.07615](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07615) (extension, not independent).
- **Atlas note:** an open-model counterpart to the Biology two-hop case study that needs no transcoder.

#### 5.4 Goodfire, *Neurons as an Interpretable Basis* (Tigges, Kowal, McGrath, Lewis, Cammarata, 10 Nov 2026) ✅
- **Source:** [goodfire.com/research/neurons-as-an-interpretable-basis](https://www.goodfire.com/research/neurons-as-an-interpretable-basis); [Neuron Atlas](https://www.goodfire.com/research/public_neuron_atlas/all_labels). No code or licence found.
- **Model:** Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (MoE, 40 layers, 256 experts/layer); labels from a fine-tuned Qwen3-8B, Claude Opus 5.5 as describer/judge.
- **Findings:** keeping only the top 320 of 4,096 routed neurons per token (ablating 92.2% of activations) moves web-text loss from 1.886 to 1.940 (KL 0.140) and costs 1.2–2.2 points on log-likelihood benchmarks (Table 1); some generative benchmarks drop much more without fine-tuning (e.g. MBPP+, GPQA-Diamond; ◐ read from a dense table), recoverable by a light fine-tune. Of 5,468 neuron "poles" (sign-separated directions), 69% have one mode carrying ≥ 90% of KL damage (Mode Statistics). Descriptions: 95% detection accuracy vs random text; simulation recovers 73% of restorable damage above the 99.9th percentile.
- **Evidence:** causal (mean ablation via loss/KL/benchmarks, activation replacement) + LLM-judge scores.
- **Critique:** testingthewaters, [*Datasets Determine the Difficulty of Interpretability*](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/EMKRpxKswPZEbP8ey/datasets-determine-the-difficulty-of-interpretability) (LessWrong, 14 Nov 2026): evaluation data too in-distribution; proposes gibberish, glitch tokens, jailbreaks and novel in-context patterns.

#### 5.5 Cammarata, *Neural Networks Are Already Clean* (15 Oct 2026) ✅
- **Source:** [already-clean.goodfire.com](https://already-clean.goodfire.com) — a **single-author essay** (Nick Cammarata, Goodfire; with C. Tigges on the language-neuron parts), self-described as partly highly speculative. No code or licence found.
- **Models:** mostly vision (InceptionV1, ConvNeXt variants, DINOv2); preliminary audits of Qwen3-32B and Kimi-K3.
- **Claims:** most apparent polysemanticity reflects weak tools and explanations rather than superposition. Qwen3-32B audit: ~90% of neuron poles have one job, ~7% two related jobs, 2–4% not understood (small samples). Share of interpretable InceptionV1 final-layer neurons rises from ~5% (2014-era tools) to 50% (better feature visualisation), 70% (retrained) and 99% in a same-size ConvNeXt. Squeezing layer 3b from 480 to 10 neurons keeps 58.35% top-1; splicing in an SAE drops top-1 58.8% → 43.0%.
- **Evidence:** mostly observational/author judgement; some causal tests (neuron ablation, steering, retraining, capacity squeeze).
- **Critiques:** [LessWrong linkpost discussion](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/x4kvabkF2Jk8GLZp8/linkpost-neural-networks-are-already-clean) (51 comments; e.g. Neel Nanda: superposition still likely for facts, weak activations matter, vision results may not transfer; Stefan Hex: compression experiments do not rule out superposition). Kamil_GR, [Habr toy replication](https://habr.com/ru/articles/1098110/): ablating weak activations in an MLP-512 classifier slightly raised average accuracy (97.5 → 98.3%) but cut boundary-case accuracy 93.1 → 72.4%. ⚠ [*Why clean neurons are not enough*](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/rfnjfHCdKJTGgLP8L/why-clean-neurons-are-not-enough) (LessWrong) could not be loaded (HTTP 401).

#### 5.6 Anthropic, *Returning to the Neuron Basis for Interpretability* (Shone & Dunlap, 23 Nov 2026) ✅
- **Source:** two posts on the Alignment Science Blog (not peer-reviewed): [Part 1, *Answering Three Objections*](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/neuron-basis-theory/) and [Part 2, *Using Neurons For Safety Applications*](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/neuron-basis-applications/). No code or licence; links Transluce's [WeirdChat](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Transluce/WeirdChat) dataset.
- **Models:** Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen3-8B/14B/32B, OLMo-3-32B, OLMo-2-7B, Zephyr-7B; 74 backdoored models, 68 LoRA adapters, 23 WMDP-cyber unlearning checkpoints.
- **Method:** each neuron's output vector is read with the J-lens (§8.1), then tested by intervention.
- **Findings:** neurons that add two unrelated concepts under one sign are ≤ ~1% of signed directions (0.4–1.1%, App. B.2); output vectors transplanted to other layers still promote their concept in ~90–98% of concept–layer pairs (Fig. 3); removing a fact takes 44–273 neurons for 15/18 facts (Table D1). Applications: zero-shot concept detection AUROC 0.903 on 500 AxBench concepts vs 0.888 best SAE and 0.875 embedding search; neuron steering elicits the target behaviour in 21% of responses vs 2% unsteered (SAE latents 3–18%); blind model audit names the quirk correctly in 40% (outputs only), 61% (+steering), 75% (+neuron readouts); 14/23 "unlearned" checkpoints still rank the correct answer first for most facts.
- **Evidence:** causal (ablation, output-vector injection, steering, patching) + correlational; sign/Fisher tests with Holm correction in places.
- **Replications:** none found (published four days before this review).

#### 6.1 Towards Monosemanticity (Bricken, Templeton, Batson et al., 2023) ✅
- **Source:** *Towards Monosemanticity: Decomposing Language Models With Dictionary Learning*, Transformer Circuits Thread, 4 Oct 2023, [transformer-circuits.pub/2023/monosemantic-features](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2023/monosemantic-features/index.html) (24 authors; no arXiv copy; no licence statement found; interactive browser over 90 dictionaries; no training code).
- **Models:** two Anthropic-internal 1-layer transformers (512-neuron ReLU MLP, residual dimension 128, 100B Pile tokens). **Not released.**
- **Findings:** SAEs on 8B MLP-activation samples with dictionaries from 512 (1×) to 131,072 (256×) features; the main analysis uses a 4,096-feature run (A/1). #### 6.2 Scaling Monosemanticity (Templeton, Conerly, Marcus et al., 2024) ✅

#### 6.4 Feature absorption (Chanin, Wilken-Smith, Dulka et al., 2024) ✅
- **Source:** *A is for Absorption: Studying Feature Splitting and Absorption in Sparse Autoencoders*, NeurIPS 2025 (oral), arXiv [2409.14507](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14507) (CC BY 4.0). Code: [lasr-spelling/sae-spelling](https://github.com/lasr-spelling/sae-spelling) (MIT); [explorer](https://feature-absorption.streamlit.app).
- **Models:** Gemma-2-2B with Gemma Scope 16K/65K residual SAEs; own SAEs on Qwen2-0.5B and Llama-3.2-1B.
- **Findings:** on a first-letter spelling task with k-sparse probes, an apparently monosemantic "starts with L" latent fails to fire on some L-words because a more specific token-level latent **absorbs** the direction. Absorption occurs in every SAE tested and increases with width and sparsity (Fig. 9b); cleanest first-letter features at L0 ≈ 50–100 (Fig. 4c). A toy model and proof show absorption lowers the sparsity loss (§3). Absorption rates are given only in figures; the metric breaks past layer 18 of Gemma-2-2B (A.12).
- **Evidence:** probes (correlational) + ablation-based absorption detection (causal) + theory.

#### 6.6 The Eiffel Tower Llama (Louapre, 2025) ✅

## 7. Steering vectors and linear directions

#### 7.1 ActAdd (Turner, Thiergart, Leech et al., 2023) ✅
- **Source:** *Steering Language Models With Activation Engineering*, arXiv [2308.10248](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10248) (v1, 20 Aug 2023, titled *Activation Addition: Steering Language Models Without Optimization*; no venue on arXiv). Code/data: [Zenodo 13879423](https://zenodo.org/records/13879423) (CC-BY-4.0); earlier [montemac/activation_additions](https://github.com/montemac/activation_additions) (MIT).
- **Models:** v1: GPT-2-XL only. v5: GPT-2-XL (topic, perplexity, ConceptNet), OPT-6.7B and LLaMA-3-8B (toxicity, sentiment), Llama-1-13B and GPT-J-6B (App. E).
- **Mechanism:** steering vector = difference of residual activations for a prompt pair at layer l, added with coefficient c at the first positions (Alg. 1, §3).
- **Numbers:** topic steering peaks at layer 6 with > 90% success vs a 2% baseline (v1 Exp. 3). The Love vector works best at layer 6 (App. C); "Love" alone vs a blank prompt is ineffective, only Love − Hate works. RealToxicityPrompts (n = 1,000; Table 3): toxicity 0.112 vs 0.134 unsteered (OPT), 0.108 vs 0.114 (LLaMA-3). Sentiment neg→pos success 0.669 vs 0.417 (LLaMA-3), 0.564 vs 0.445 (OPT) (Table 4). App. E.3's "21% absolute increase" for OPT does not match Table 4 (~12 points).
- **Caveat:** the paper itself gives no layer/coefficient for GPT-2-XL Love − Hate; the commonly cited "layer 6, c = 5" comes from the earlier LessWrong post (⚠ not loaded) and a Llama-13B appendix table.
- **Evidence:** causal (activation addition with layer/coefficient sweeps).

#### 7.2 Representation Engineering (Zou, Phan, Chen et al., 2023) ✅
- **Source:** *Representation Engineering: A Top-Down Approach to AI Transparency*, arXiv [2310.01405](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01405). Code: [andyzoujm/representation-engineering](https://github.com/andyzoujm/representation-engineering) (MIT).
- **Models:** LLaMA-2-Chat 7B/13B/70B, Vicuna-33B-Uncensored, others.
- **Mechanism:** Linear Artificial Tomography (LAT): design stimuli, collect (usually last-token) activations, take PCA of paired differences; PC1 is the "reading vector" (§3.1.1).
- **Numbers:** TruthfulQA MC1 best-LAT 58.9 / 54.2 / 69.8 for 7B / 13B / 70B vs zero-shot 31.0 / 35.9 / 29.9 (Table 1; ◐ column order inferred from a flattened table). Honesty control (Table 2, 7B / 13B): contrast vector 47.9 / 54.0 vs baseline 31.0 / 35.9, ActAdd 33.7 / 38.8, LoRRA 42.3 / 47.5.
- **Evidence:** correlational (reading, lie detection) + causal (vector addition, LoRRA fine-tuning).
- **Replication:** Im & Li ([2502.02716](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02716)) — **partial**: PCA (RepE-style) vectors underperform difference-of-means on Llama-2-7B-Chat multiple choice (e.g. corrigibility 85.74 diff-of-means vs 64.32 PCA).

#### 7.3 Refusal direction (Arditi, Obeso, Syed et al., 2024) ✅
- **Source:** *Refusal in Language Models Is Mediated by a Single Direction*, NeurIPS 2024 (venue confirmed by search only), arXiv [2406.11717](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11717). Code: [andyrdt/refusal_direction](https://github.com/andyrdt/refusal_direction) (Apache-2.0).
- **Models:** 13 chat models, 1.8B–72B: Qwen 1.8/7/14/72B, Yi 6/34B, Gemma 2/7B, Llama-2 7/13/70B, Llama-3 8/70B (Table 1).
- **Mechanism:** difference-in-means of harmful vs harmless activations per layer and post-instruction position (§2.3); selected by refusal induction, KL < 0.1 on harmless prompts, layer < 0.8L (App. C). Directional ablation at all layers removes refusal; adding the direction induces it; weight orthogonalisation is equivalent to directional ablation (§4, App. E).
- **Numbers:** HarmBench attack success with orthogonalised weights, with system prompt (without): Llama-2-7B 22.6 (79.9), Llama-2-70B 4.4 (62.9), Qwen-7B 79.2 (74.8), Qwen-72B 78.0 (79.2) (Table 2). Capability cost small, e.g. Llama-3-70B MMLU −0.1, GSM8K −0.4, TruthfulQA −2.3 (Table 3); TruthfulQA drops consistently. Main refusal/safety scores (Figs. 1, 3; n = 100) are only plotted.
- **Evidence:** causal (directional ablation, activation addition, weight orthogonalisation).
- **Replications / critiques:** Labonne, [*Uncensor any LLM with abliteration*](https://huggingface.co/blog/mlabonne/abliteration) — **succeeded** on a Llama-3-8B derivative with benchmark drops; thousands of community "abliterated" models on HF ([search](https://huggingface.co/models?search=abliterated); 9,595 listed on 2026-11-27, outcomes unchecked). Wang et al. ([2505.17306](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17306)) — **succeeded**: an English refusal vector transfers across 14 languages. Zhang, [*Does Safety Live in a Shared Multilingual Subspace?*](https://aclanthology.org/2026.mrl-1.19/) (MRL 2026) — **failed** cross-lingually on Qwen2.5-7B and Llama-3.2-3B (ablating the English direction removes 61.9 pp of English refusal but changes refusal in 0/9 other languages). Ivanova ([2610.28897](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28897), BlackboxNLP 2026 reproducibility) — **partial**: universality "graded rather than binary" on Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct. Wollschläger et al. ([2502.17420](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17420), ICML 2025 per search) — **refinement**: multiple independent refusal directions and concept cones of up to five dimensions on Gemma-2, Qwen-2.5 and Llama-3; gradient-based directions reduce TruthfulQA damage by ~40%.

#### 7.4 Persona vectors (Chen, Arditi, Sleight et al., 2025) ✅
- **Source:** *Persona Vectors: Monitoring and Controlling Character Traits in Language Models*, arXiv [2507.21509](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.21509) (Anthropic Fellows). Code: [safety-research/persona_vectors](https://github.com/safety-research/persona_vectors) (Apache-2.0).
- **Models:** Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct.
- **Mechanism:** from a trait name and description, an automated pipeline builds 5 contrastive system-prompt pairs and 40 questions; the vector is the difference of mean residual activations over response tokens (§2).
- **Numbers:** last-prompt-token projection predicts later trait expression at r = 0.75–0.83 (§3.3, Fig. 4). Fine-tuning shift along the vector correlates with trait expression at r = 0.76–0.97 (cross-trait baselines 0.34–0.86; §4.2). "Preventative steering" during training limits trait shifts while keeping coherence > 80 and preserving MMLU better than inference-time steering (§5.2, Fig. 7B). Projection-difference flags problematic training samples in LMSYS-Chat-1M (§6).
- **Evidence:** causal (inference-time and training-time steering) + correlational (monitoring, data flagging).
- **Replication:** dgom, [*Do persona vectors detect implicitly-elicited traits?*](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/aCxCkzPoRKHxkqsML/do-persona-vectors-detect-implicitly-elicited-traits-1) (LessWrong, 29 Aug 2026) — **succeeded** on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (r = 0.840 evil, 0.795 sycophancy, 0.743 hallucination); **partial** for situational elicitation.

#### 7.5 The Assistant Axis (Lu, Gallagher, Michala, Fish, Lindsey, 2026) ✅
- **Source:** *The Assistant Axis: Situating and Stabilizing the Default Persona of Language Models*, arXiv [2601.10387](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10387) (15 Jan 2026, CC BY 4.0; MATS + Anthropic Fellows); [Anthropic post](https://www.anthropic.com/research/assistant-axis); [Neuronpedia demo](https://neuronpedia.org/assistant-axis). Code: [safety-research/assistant-axis](https://github.com/safety-research/assistant-axis) (**no licence file**). ICML 2026 spotlight claimed but the page returned 404 (⚠).
- **Models:** Gemma-2-27B, Qwen3-32B (thinking off), Llama-3.3-70B; base Gemma-2-27B and Llama-3.1-70B.
- **Mechanism:** 275 roles × 5 system prompts × 240 questions give role vectors; PCA needs 4–19 components for 70% variance (§2.1). The axis = mean default-Assistant activation − mean of fully role-playing vectors; cosine with PC1 > 0.60 at all layers (§3.1).
- **Numbers:** persona jailbreaks succeed 65.3–88.5% (§3.2.1). First-turn projection correlates with second-turn harmful responses at r = 0.39–0.52 (§4.3). Activation capping at the 25th-percentile projection (Qwen layers 46–53, Llama 56–71) cuts harmful responses by nearly 60% in the paper (the blog says roughly 50%) without loss on IFEval, MMLU-Pro, GSM8K, EQ-Bench (§5).
- **Evidence:** causal (addition along the axis, activation capping) + correlational (drift monitoring).
- **Replications:** none found.

#### 7.6 Function vectors (Todd, Li, Sen Sharma et al., 2023) ✅
- **Source:** *Function Vectors in Large Language Models*, ICLR 2024, arXiv [2310.15213](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15213); [project page](https://functions.baulab.info). Code: [ericwtodd/function_vectors](https://github.com/ericwtodd/function_vectors) (MIT).
- **Models:** GPT-J-6B, GPT-NeoX-20B, Llama-2 7B/13B/70B.
- **Mechanism:** rank heads by average indirect effect on shuffled-label ICL prompts; the FV is the sum of the top heads' mean task-conditioned outputs (Eq. 5; 10 heads for GPT-J … 100 for 70B), added at ~⅓ depth.
- **Numbers:** zero-shot accuracy averaged over 6 tasks rises 5.5 → 57.5% (GPT-J), 6.7 → 57.1% (NeoX), 8.2 → 83.8% (Llama-2-70B) (Table 2); natural-text antonym template (GPT-J) 2.4 → 52.7% (Table 3).
- **Evidence:** causal (mediation analysis + FV addition).
- **Related:** Hendel et al., *In-Context Learning Creates Task Vectors* ([2310.15916](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15916), Findings of EMNLP 2023): task-vector patching keeps ~80–90% of ICL accuracy over 7 models and 18 tasks.

#### 7.7 Steering reliability (verified, one line each)
- **CAA**, Panickssery et al. ([2312.06681](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06681), code [nrimsky/CAA](https://github.com/nrimsky/CAA), MIT): diff-of-means at the A/B answer token on Llama-2-7B/13B-Chat, 7 behaviours, no significant MMLU effect.
- **Tan et al.** ([2407.12404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12404)): on 40 Model-Written-Evals datasets several produce the opposite of the intended behaviour for almost half of inputs; in- vs out-of-distribution steerability correlates ρ = 0.891 / 0.694 (Llama-2-7B-Chat / Qwen-1.5-14B-Chat).
- **Im & Li** ([2502.02716](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02716)): difference-of-means beats PCA and classifier directions on multiple-choice steering.
- **AxBench** (§6.5): prompting beats all representation-steering methods on Gemma-2.
- **SteerScope**, Yang, Jiang, Wu et al., *Does Steering Break Your Model?* ([2610.07722](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07722), 6 Oct 2026): 23 methods × 15 metrics; activation steering does not yet beat prompt steering, and efficacy trades off against side effects.

## 8. Lenses, the global workspace, introspection and NLAs

#### 8.1 J-lens / global workspace (Gurnee, Sofroniew, Pearce et al., 2026) ✅
- **Source:** *Verbalizable Representations Form a Global Workspace in Language Models*, Transformer Circuits Thread, [transformer-circuits.pub/2026/workspace](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/workspace/index.html); [Anthropic post, 6 Jul 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/research/global-workspace); arXiv [2607.15495](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15495) (16 Jul 2026; 16 authors, Lindsey last). Code: [anthropics/jacobian-lens](https://github.com/anthropics/jacobian-lens) (Apache-2.0; reference code, installs as `jlens` from source). Pre-fitted open-model lenses: [neuronpedia/jacobian-lens](https://huggingface.co/neuronpedia/jacobian-lens).
- **Models:** Claude Sonnet 4.5 (default), Haiku 4.5, Opus 4.5 (some analyses Opus 4.6). **Closed → redraw; the paper itself studies no open model.**
- **What the J-lens computes (§2.1):** J_ℓ = E[∂h_final,t′ / ∂h_ℓ,t], the Jacobian of the **final-layer residual stream** (not the logits) with respect to layer ℓ, averaged over positions t′ ≥ t and 1,000 pretraining-like prompts; readout softmax(W_U · norm(J_ℓ h_ℓ)). With J_ℓ = I it reduces to the logit lens.
- **Findings:** a coherent "workspace" band from ~L38 to ~L92 on a 0–100 depth scale (§4.1); occupancy plateaus at ~25 vectors and excess variance explained never exceeds 10% (§4.2, Fig. 30). Swapping J-space components moves the verbal report to the target (top-5) on 59% of trials vs 5% for non-J components (§3.1, Fig. 8). Two-hop swaps succeed 54% / 70% / 70% on Haiku / Sonnet / Opus 4.5 (§3.3). Audit: ablating eval-awareness directions cuts verbalised eval awareness 71% → 3% while blackmail rises 0/180 → 13/180 rollouts (§5.1, Fig. 36).
- **Evidence:** mostly causal (coordinate swaps, ablation, injection, clamping) + correlational lens readouts.

#### 8.2 J++ lens (Ayonrinde & Lindsey, Oct 2026) ✅
- **Source:** *J++ Lens: Jacobian Filtering Enables More Faithful Workspace Lenses*, [LessWrong, 8 Oct 2026](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/nc9dHfcB22JdMzGbr/j-lens-jacobian-filtering-enables-more-faithful-workspace) (Anthropic Fellows) and [Circuits Updates — October 2026](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/october-update/index.html). **Not an arXiv v2.** Code: [safety-research/jpp_lens](https://github.com/safety-research/jpp_lens) (Apache-2.0); lenses [koayon/jpp-lenses](https://huggingface.co/koayon/jpp-lenses) (apache-2.0).
- **Models (open):** Qwen3.6-27B (main), Qwen3.5-9B, Gemma-4-31B, OLMo-3-32B, Qwen3.5-122B-A10B, DeepSeek-V4-Flash.
- **Method:** still one linear map per layer (drop-in for the J-lens), with (i) **Jacobian Filtering** — k-means clusters (E = 8) with one "expert" Jacobian each, combined with learned signed weights; (ii) an LRP-style backward pass from the R-lens; (iii) readout filtering of non-alphanumeric tokens.
- **Numbers:** recall@10 on Qwen3.6-27B 55.2% vs 35.7% (J-lens) and 37.7% (R-lens) (Fig. 1a); removing Jacobian Filtering costs 11.7 pp, 60% of the 19.5 pp gain (Table 2).
- **Evidence:** correlational readout evals + causal lens-vector ablation in a case study. No independent replication found.

## 9. Synthesis: what transfers to open models, and what the atlas should show

**What replicates robustly.** Circuits found by patching in GPT-2-scale models re-appear across seeds, sizes and families: induction heads (§1.2), IOI (§1.3), copy suppression (§1.5), greater-than (§1.6) and successor heads (§1.8) all have successful replications in Pythia or other open models, with the caveat that the *algorithm* is stable while the *specific heads* that implement it change during training ([Tigges et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10827)). Single linear directions found by difference of means also transfer well within a model family: refusal (§7.3), persona traits (§7.4), truth (§3.8) and emotional valence (§7b.2).

**What does not (yet) replicate on small open models.** Three high-profile closed-model mechanisms have not been reproduced: newline-site rhyme planning (§2.3–2.5, where the effective site is the final prompt token), the 36 + 59 parallel-path addition circuit in transcoder graphs (§2.3), and J-space selectivity / workspace structure below ~9B parameters (§8.3). Emotion arousal geometry and emotion-steering effects on behaviour are weak or reversed in open models (§7b.2). Introspection replications range from success to null (§8.4).

**Recurring methodological lessons.**
1. *Results depend on the decomposition.* Attribution-graph conclusions change with the transcoder (§2.5); SAEs dilute or shatter manifolds (§4.5, §4.1) and absorb features (§6.4); CLT features carry only part of the steering signal that raw activations carry (§4.2 replication).
2. *Results depend on the metric.* IOI faithfulness moves with ablation choices (§1.3); the docstring circuit's recovered performance depends on the baseline (§1.7); manifold-steering energy vs task accuracy can disagree (§4.2 replications).
3. *Correlational geometry needs baselines.* Static word embeddings already explain much of space/time decoding (§3.7) and part of emotion geometry (§7b.2); whitening in high dimensions creates orthogonality for random concepts (§3.9); nucleotide statistics confound the phylogeny manifold (§3.5).
4. *Smooth is not continuous.* Late-layer steering looks smooth even when early-layer behaviour is discrete (§4.3); curved activations can coexist with linear computation (§3.11; §4.4 base-10 addition rather than circle rotation).
5. *Simple baselines are strong.* Prompting beats SAE and most representation steering (§6.5, §6.6, §7.7); difference-of-means beats PCA directions (§7.2).

- *Recompute on GPT-2 small / attn-only models:* IOI, greater-than, copy suppression, docstring, induction heads, universal and entropy neurons, toy superposition, Othello-style probing (retrained small model). GPT-2 small barely solves weekday arithmetic (§3.1) and has a weak counting-manifold probe (§3.3), so show geometry there with that caveat.

- *Recompute on Gemma-2-2B / Qwen3-1.7B with circuit-tracer:* Dallas → Austin and multilingual graphs (replicated), poem planning (show the final-token result next to the redrawn Haiku newline result), entity-recognition latents.

- *Every entry should carry:* evidence type (causal vs correlational), the dictionary used (SAE/transcoder/neurons/none), and the replication verdict with links.

## 11. Corrections to the task brief

Things found while reading the sources that contradict or refine the brief that commissioned this review:

1. **Jacopin 2604.15010 and 2609.18440** are single-author papers (Éric Jacopin), and **neither builds attribution graphs**: both are CLT-feature steering and activation-patching stress tests of Biology's poem-planning result; 2609.18440 does not recover the newline planning site (§2.4–2.5).
2. **Neuronpedia/circuit-tracer Gemma-2-2B replications** exist for Dallas → Austin (replicated) and multilingual (mostly), but **not for rhyme planning** (only a one-token rhyme graph) **or 36 + 59 addition** (none found) (§2.3).
3. **Goodfire's greater-than CLT replication** is on GPT-2 small, is a blog post only (no arXiv, no code), and reports attribution heatmaps without interventions (§2.7).
4. **IOI head count:** arXiv v1 says 26 heads; the ICLR 2023 abstract says 28 (§1.3).
5. **Successor heads have no public code** (cited repo 404) and study only Llama-2-**7B**, not several Llama-2 sizes (§1.8).
6. **Copy suppression** was published at BlackboxNLP 2024 under a different title (§1.5). **MIB uses none** of copy suppression, greater-than, docstring or successor heads as tasks (§1.8).
7. **Curve Circuits** was published 30 Jan 2021 (URL says 2020); *High-Low Frequency Detectors* says fifteen neurons but lists 14 (§1.9–1.10). The Gradient essay's title is *Do Large Language Models learn world models or just surface statistics?* (§1.12).
8. **Engels et al.** current title is *Not All Language Model Features Are One-Dimensionally Linear* (§3.1). **Kantamneni & Tegmark** is an ICLR 2025 workshop paper with no released code (§3.2).
9. **The T-Tech counting-manifold replication** lives at `corl-team/counting_manifolds` (Space `t-tech/manifolds`) and is correlational only (no ringing, boundary heads or interventions) (§3.3).
10. **arXiv 2603.12261 (LCS)** is the *Latent Color Subspace* of the **FLUX.1 diffusion model**, not a language model (§3.12).
11. **Goodfire phylogeny manifold**: the page does not state the Evo 2 size; evidence is correlational with an acknowledged nucleotide-statistics confound (§3.5).
12. **Toy Models of Superposition**: no licence on the transformer-circuits page; arXiv copy under the arXiv non-exclusive licence (§3.4). **Towards Monosemanticity has no arXiv copy** (§6.1).
13. **Marks & Tegmark** study only LLaMA-2 models; **Park et al. 2406.01506** use Gemma-**2B** (Gemma 1), not Gemma-2; **Shai et al.** link no code (§3.6, §3.8, §3.9).
14. **2609.37680** studies a comparison task in Qwen2.5-7B and is a counterpoint, not a refutation, of the linebreaking paper (§3.11).
15. **Goodfire Neural Geometry series:** every arXiv title differs from its blog title; "Geometric Calculator" = *Arithmetic in the Wild*, whose mechanism is **base-10 addition with Fourier number circles**, not rotation on a months circle; *Spokes, not a Ring* is by Pivotal Research, is not on the Goodfire hub, and is a narrower critique (§4).
16. ***Neural Networks Are Already Clean*** is a single-author essay by Nick Cammarata with mostly vision evidence (§5.5). **Anthropic's *Returning to the Neuron Basis*** is two posts by Shone & Dunlap dated 23 Nov 2026 (§5.6).
17. **Scaling Monosemanticity arXiv 2605.29358** is CC BY 4.0, posted by co-author Adam Pearce; Golden Gate Claude is a separate demo, not in the paper (§6.2). **Gemma Scope 2** (Dec 2025) exists and includes CLTs (§6.3).
18. **ActAdd**: the paper gives no layer/coefficient for GPT-2-XL Love − Hate; v1 had a different title (§7.1). **Assistant Axis**: paper says "nearly 60%" harm reduction, blog "roughly 50%"; code repo has no licence (§7.5).

There are **two** GPT-2 attempts (one failed/unresolved, one finding no workspace structure) (§8.1–8.3). Introspection also has an arXiv copy, [2601.01828](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.01828) (§8.4).
