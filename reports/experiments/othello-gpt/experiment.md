## Question

Does the open Othello-GPT checkpoint represent the board linearly as mine / theirs / empty (Nanda, Lee, Wattenberg 2023,
[arXiv 2309.00941](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00941)) and can editing that representation change its predicted legal
moves (Li et al. 2023, [arXiv 2210.13382](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13382))?

## Setup

- **Model:** TransformerLens `othello-gpt` (8 layers, d_model 512), trained by Li et al. on synthetic random legal games.
- **Data:** 3,000 training + 500 test games generated by our engine (uniformly random legal moves, like the synthetic
  dataset), 59 move tokens each.
- **Probes:** per residual-stream layer (0 = embeddings), a linear softmax probe W ∈ R^{d×64×3} trained with AdamW on
  cross-entropy for {mine, theirs, empty} (relative to the player who just moved) and {black, white, empty} (absolute);
  accuracy over all 64 squares of held-out games.
- **Intervention:** at the best middle block (chosen among 3–5 by probe accuracy), set the residual's component along
  one occupied square's mine/theirs probe direction to the opposite class's value, keeping only flips that change the
  legal-move set; error = Li et al.'s count (take the model's top-k squares, k = number of legal moves of the edited
  board, and count mismatches); 150 interventions.

**Controls.** Probes on a randomly initialised model; probes with shuffled labels; a random direction of the same norm
for the intervention; the unedited model's error against the edited board.

**Why these settings.** Layer 5–6 is where Nanda et al. find the cleanest linear board; 3,000 games × 59 positions ≈
177k examples per square make probes stable.

## Interpretation

The model almost never predicts illegal moves. The relative (mine/theirs) board is linearly decodable at ~99% while the
absolute black/white board is much lower, reproducing Nanda et al.'s key observation. Shuffled labels stay at the
majority-class level. The random-init control was an all-zero network until the methods review (TransformerLens
pretrained configs set `init_weights=False`); properly initialised, it reaches about 72%, because occupancy is partly
decodable from token and position embeddings alone. The trained model still beats it by more than 25 points, so the
verdict is unchanged. Editing the board representation halves the disagreement
with the edited board's legal moves while a random direction changes nothing; the effect is clearly causal but weaker
than Li et al.'s (their error 0.10 vs 2.72), probably because we edit one layer with a simple linear flip.

## Deviations from the paper

- Linear probes (Li et al. used non-linear probes; Nanda et al. linear) and a single-layer edit.

## Limitations

- Synthetic games only; championship-game behaviour not tested.
